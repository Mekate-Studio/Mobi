## Why

Slice 4 records dependency inputs and missing evidence, but a candidate cannot
be assessed causally without a passing baseline in independent owned resources.
Maintenance needs durable failure and recovery records before real Kotlin
Toolchain rehearsal can safely reuse a common executor.

## What Changes

- Add a Ruby standard-library executor with a versioned adapter/check protocol,
  explicit source/patch/tool/policy identities and baseline-first execution.
- Journal owned copies, caches, check processes and cleanup before creation;
  retain failed/interrupted runs and refuse unproven process/resource ownership.
- Add inspect/recover and dry-run-first cleanup with leases, retention and holds.
- Exercise independent Kotlin and Elixir fixture adapters without native tools,
  a backend, databases, provider credentials or dependency adoption.
- Integrate contracts through the existing repository-owned quality job and
  document measured results, unsupported resources and the next native slice.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities

- `maintenance-execution`: Isolated baseline/candidate checks, durable evidence,
  verified ownership, failure recovery and conservative cleanup.

### Modified Capabilities

None. Existing inventory and pre-commit/native execution paths remain intact.

## Impact

Maintenance scripts, a separate execution policy, fixture adapters, contract tests
and public English documentation. No new dependencies, schedules, upgrades,
bridge changes, application/backend scaffolding, commits or publishing. Actual
Toolchain graphs/native rehearsal and database/simulator resources remain later
adapter work; this slice proves the common protocol with executable fixtures.
